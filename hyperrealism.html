Hyperreality

Pushing the edge of what's real and what's not using detail, texture, color, and lighting.

The History of Hyperreality

Broken down into 3 stages, pre-digital imaging, during the rise of digital imaging, and current day.

Pre-Digital Imaging

Before the rise of digital imaging, hyperrealism was a style of painting and sculpture that focused on creating works that were extremely realistic and detailed. Artists would use techniques such as photorealism and trompe-l'oeil to create works that were almost indistinguishable from photographs.

During the Rise of Digital Imaging

With the rise of digital imaging, hyperrealism has evolved to include digital art and photography. Artists can now use software to create works that are even more realistic and detailed than ever before. This has led to a new wave of hyperrealistic art that pushes the boundaries of what is possible.

Current Day

Today, hyperrealism continues to be a popular style in both traditional and digital art. Artists are constantly pushing the limits of what is possible, creating works that are more realistic and detailed than ever before. Hyperrealism has become a way for artists to explore the relationship between reality and perception, challenging viewers to question what they see.
